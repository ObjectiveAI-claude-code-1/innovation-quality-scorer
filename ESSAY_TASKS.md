# Innovation Quality Scorer: Task Definitions

This document defines the tasks that comprise the Innovation Quality Scorer function. Each task evaluates a specific dimension of innovation quality, and together they produce a comprehensive assessment of a startup idea's innovative merit.

---

## Task 1: Conceptual Novelty — Core Idea Originality

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the core concept represents a genuine departure from existing approaches or merely iterates on established patterns.

**Description:** This task assesses the fundamental originality of the idea's central concept. It distinguishes between pure derivatives (applying an existing successful concept to a new vertical with minimal adaptation), meaningful variations (introducing substantive modifications that address real limitations), and category creators (defining entirely new problem spaces or solution categories).

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Has this exact concept been attempted before? If so, what makes this version meaningfully different?
- Does the idea reveal something hidden—a fresh perspective that was always there but unnoticed?
- Would a knowledgeable observer in the domain be genuinely surprised by the insight, not just the audacity?
- Does the idea expand the space of what's possible, potentially enabling follow-on innovation?

**Response Options:**
- Category Creator: Defines an entirely new problem space or solution category; genuinely unprecedented approach
- Meaningful Variation: Takes existing concepts but introduces substantive modifications that address real limitations or expand possibilities significantly
- Minor Variation: Adapts existing concepts with some modifications, but the core approach remains familiar
- Pure Derivative: Applies an existing successful concept to a new vertical with minimal adaptation ("X for Y")
- Redundant: Essentially identical to existing solutions with no meaningful differentiation

---

## Task 2: Conceptual Novelty — Perceived vs. Actual Novelty

**Purpose:** Distinguish between ideas that are genuinely novel versus ideas that merely feel novel due to the founder's limited research.

**Description:** Many ideas feel novel to their creators simply because the creator hasn't done sufficient research. What seems like a breakthrough insight may be a well-known idea in adjacent fields, a failed startup from the previous decade, or a current competitor. This task assesses whether the idea's novelty is genuine or a function of insufficient market awareness.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch demonstrate awareness of prior art, competitors, and related approaches?
- Is there evidence that the founder has researched the landscape thoroughly?
- Does the idea acknowledge and address why previous similar attempts failed or succeeded?
- Would an industry expert recognize this as genuinely new, or as reinventing the wheel?

**Response Options:**
- Genuinely Novel: Demonstrates deep awareness of the landscape and presents something authentically new
- Likely Novel: Shows reasonable awareness of competitors and prior art; novelty claim is credible
- Uncertain Novelty: Limited evidence of landscape research; novelty may or may not be genuine
- Probably Not Novel: Shows signs of reinventing existing solutions without awareness
- Almost Certainly Not Novel: Clearly describes something that already exists with no apparent awareness

---

## Task 3: Technical Innovation — Enabling Technology Recognition

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea leverages a newly-crossed technology threshold that makes something possible now that wasn't before.

**Description:** Some innovations become possible only when underlying technology reaches a threshold—smartphones enabling Uber, cloud computing enabling SaaS, LLMs enabling new AI applications. This task assesses whether the idea demonstrates recognition of such a threshold and moves decisively to exploit it.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Is there a specific technology, cost decline, or infrastructure development that enables this idea now?
- Does the timing appear right—neither too early (technology immature) nor too late (already exploited)?
- Is the founder articulating why "now is the time" with technical specificity?
- Does the idea require technology that doesn't yet exist, or does it leverage recently-available capabilities?

**Response Options:**
- Perfect Timing: Identifies a recently-crossed threshold and moves decisively; compelling "why now" explanation
- Good Timing: Leverages relatively new technology capabilities with reasonable timing justification
- Neutral Timing: Uses existing technology without clear "why now"; could have been built years ago
- Questionable Timing: Relies on technology that may not be ready or is already mature/commoditized
- Poor Timing: Depends on technology that doesn't exist or proposes something already saturated

---

## Task 4: Technical Innovation — Novel Application or Breakthrough

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea involves applying existing technology to new domains in innovative ways, or represents a genuine technical breakthrough.

**Description:** Technical innovation can manifest as novel application (repurposing proven technology for underserved domains with unexpected synergies) or technical breakthrough (new algorithms, materials, or processes). This task assesses the technical innovation dimension of the idea.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the idea apply existing technology to new domains in ways that reveal unexpected synergies?
- Is there a genuine technical advance—new algorithms, materials, or processes?
- Does the technical approach create emergent capabilities through novel integration?
- Is the technical claim plausible and specific, or vague and hand-wavy?

**Response Options:**
- Technical Breakthrough: Proposes genuine technical advance with specific, credible claims
- Novel Integration: Combines existing technologies in ways that create emergent capabilities
- Novel Application: Applies proven technology to underserved domains with clear synergies
- Standard Application: Uses technology in conventional ways without technical innovation
- No Technical Element: No meaningful technology component or entirely conventional technology use

---

## Task 5: Business Model Innovation — Value Capture Mechanism

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea introduces innovative approaches to capturing value.

**Description:** Business model innovation can be as powerful as technical innovation. This task assesses whether the idea proposes novel value capture mechanisms—new approaches to freemium, subscription, marketplace dynamics, or other revenue models that weren't previously applied or were applied in innovative ways.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the idea propose a novel way of capturing value from the solution?
- Is the monetization approach well-suited to the problem and user base?
- Does the business model create structural advantages (network effects, data moats, switching costs)?
- Is the business model coherent and defensible, or does it rely on "we'll monetize later" thinking?

**Response Options:**
- Highly Innovative: Proposes genuinely novel value capture mechanism or applies existing models in unprecedented ways
- Moderately Innovative: Adapts proven models with meaningful modifications suited to the domain
- Standard but Appropriate: Uses conventional models that fit the business well
- Conventional: Default monetization approach with no apparent innovation
- Problematic: Relies on vague or unrealistic monetization assumptions

---

## Task 6: Business Model Innovation — Value Chain Restructuring

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea reorganizes who does what in a value chain in innovative ways.

**Description:** Some innovations restructure value chains—through vertical integration, disaggregation, or platforms that coordinate distributed participants. This task assesses whether the idea proposes meaningful restructuring of how value flows through a market.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the idea restructure who does what in the value chain?
- Does it create new relationships between producers, consumers, and intermediaries?
- Does it disintermediate existing players or create new intermediary positions?
- Does it better align incentives among value chain participants?

**Response Options:**
- Significant Restructuring: Fundamentally reorganizes the value chain; creates new participant relationships
- Moderate Restructuring: Meaningfully changes value flow or participant roles
- Minor Restructuring: Makes some adjustments to conventional value chain structures
- No Restructuring: Operates within existing value chain structures
- Not Applicable: The concept doesn't involve value chain considerations

---

## Task 7: Insight Depth — User Understanding

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea reflects deep understanding of users and their needs.

**Description:** The most innovative founders understand not just what users say they want but what they actually need—and crucially, why current solutions fail to meet those needs. This task assesses the depth of user insight evident in the idea.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch include specific, non-obvious observations about user behavior?
- Is there evidence of lived experience with the problem or deep user research?
- Does it articulate why users aren't satisfied with current solutions at a deep level?
- Does it distinguish between what users say they want and what they actually need?

**Response Options:**
- Profound Understanding: Demonstrates deep, specific insight into user psychology and unmet needs; likely has lived experience
- Strong Understanding: Shows specific user insights that go beyond surface observations
- Adequate Understanding: Describes users reasonably but without surprising depth
- Surface Understanding: Generic description of users without specific insight
- Poor Understanding: Naive or stereotyped view of users; no evidence of real understanding

---

## Task 8: Insight Depth — Market Dynamics Comprehension

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea reflects understanding of why the market looks the way it does.

**Description:** Deep insight includes understanding the forces that shaped the current landscape, constraints that locked in current solutions, and changes that might unlock new possibilities. This task assesses market-level insight.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch explain why the market currently looks the way it does?
- Is there understanding of what constraints have locked in current solutions?
- Does it recognize timing factors and enabling conditions for change?
- Does it show nuanced views on competitive dynamics and incumbent behavior?

**Response Options:**
- Deep Comprehension: Sophisticated understanding of market forces, constraints, and dynamics; compelling analysis
- Good Comprehension: Solid grasp of market structure with some non-obvious insights
- Basic Comprehension: Reasonable market description without deep analysis
- Surface Comprehension: Generic market statements without real insight
- No Comprehension: Ignores market realities or makes naive assumptions about market dynamics

---

## Task 9: Insight Depth — Understanding of Previous Attempts

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea demonstrates awareness of why previous attempts in this space failed or succeeded.

**Description:** Deep insight requires understanding the landscape well enough to know why others who tried similar things succeeded or failed. This distinguishes founders who have done their homework from those who are unknowingly repeating history.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch reference previous attempts to solve this problem?
- Is there understanding of why those attempts failed or succeeded?
- Does the idea address the specific failure modes of previous attempts?
- Is there evidence of learning from the history of this problem space?

**Response Options:**
- Thorough Analysis: Deep awareness of prior attempts with specific learnings incorporated
- Good Awareness: References relevant prior attempts and addresses their outcomes
- Some Awareness: Mentions competition or prior art without deep analysis
- Limited Awareness: Little evidence of understanding prior attempts
- No Awareness: Appears unaware of relevant prior attempts or competitor history

---

## Task 10: Cliché Avoidance — Format and Framing Clichés

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea avoids tired formulations and framings that signal derivative thinking.

**Description:** Clichés like "X for Y," "AI-powered [commodity]," and "The [successful company] of [industry]" often signal ideas in crowded spaces with insufficient differentiation. This task assesses whether the pitch avoids these format clichés.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch avoid "X for Y" formulations?
- Does it avoid gratuitous buzzwords ("AI-powered," "blockchain-enabled," "disruptive")?
- Is the framing specific to this idea rather than a template that could apply to many startups?
- Are comparisons to other companies illuminating or lazy shortcuts?

**Response Options:**
- Completely Fresh: No clichéd formulations; unique and specific framing throughout
- Mostly Fresh: Avoids major clichés with perhaps minor conventional language
- Somewhat Clichéd: Contains some clichéd elements but also specific content
- Heavily Clichéd: Relies on multiple clichéd formulations and buzzwords
- Entirely Clichéd: Pure startup template language with no specific substance

---

## Task 11: Cliché Avoidance — Problem and Solution Clichés

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the problem statement and proposed solution avoid tired patterns.

**Description:** Problem clichés include "[Industry] hasn't innovated in decades" and "The market is fragmented." Solution clichés include "We're building a platform" and "We leverage AI and machine learning." This task assesses substance beneath the language.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Is the problem statement specific and insightful rather than generic?
- Does the solution description convey specific value rather than abstract buzzwords?
- Would removing buzzwords hollow out the pitch, or is there substance beneath?
- Does the pitch reveal unique insight rather than assembling familiar patterns?

**Response Options:**
- Substantive Throughout: Problem and solution are described with specificity and genuine insight
- Mostly Substantive: Some clichéd elements but core content is specific and meaningful
- Mixed: Contains both substantive elements and clichéd patterns
- Mostly Superficial: Relies primarily on clichéd problem/solution descriptions
- Entirely Superficial: Pure buzzwords and patterns with no discernible substance

---

## Task 12: First-Principles Thinking — Assumption Questioning

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the idea stems from questioning fundamental assumptions rather than accepting conventional wisdom.

**Description:** First-principles thinkers actively identify and challenge assumptions underlying current approaches. This task assesses whether there's evidence of such questioning in the idea.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch explicitly question assumptions that others take for granted?
- Is there evidence of asking "why" repeatedly to get to fundamental truths?
- Does it challenge conventional wisdom about how things must work in this domain?
- Does it identify hidden assumptions that constrain current solutions?

**Response Options:**
- Deep Questioning: Explicitly challenges fundamental assumptions with compelling reasoning
- Significant Questioning: Questions important assumptions though perhaps not the deepest ones
- Some Questioning: Shows some willingness to challenge convention
- Minimal Questioning: Largely accepts conventional assumptions with minor challenges
- No Questioning: Entirely accepts conventional framing and assumptions

---

## Task 13: First-Principles Thinking — Building from Fundamentals

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the solution is derived from fundamental truths rather than analogies to existing solutions.

**Description:** First-principles thinking builds solutions from basic truths about user needs, technology capabilities, and economic realities. This task assesses whether the solution demonstrates such derivation or relies on analogical reasoning.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Can the founder explain why their solution follows from basic truths about user needs?
- Is there reasoning from physics, economics, or psychology rather than just analogy?
- Does the solution feel derived from fundamentals or borrowed from successful patterns?
- Is there evidence of "if we were starting from scratch" thinking?

**Response Options:**
- Clearly First-Principles: Solution demonstrably derived from fundamental truths with clear reasoning chain
- Mostly First-Principles: Strong evidence of fundamental reasoning with some analogical elements
- Mixed Approach: Combines first-principles elements with analogical reasoning
- Mostly Analogical: Primarily reasons from analogy to existing solutions
- Purely Analogical: Entirely based on "X for Y" thinking with no fundamental reasoning evident

---

## Task 14: First-Principles Thinking — Understanding of Existing Landscape

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether there's sufficient understanding of why current solutions look the way they do to enable meaningful critique.

**Description:** First-principles thinking requires understanding the existing landscape well enough to critique it. Without understanding why things are the way they are, "disruption" is just uninformed arrogance.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the pitch demonstrate understanding of why incumbents operate the way they do?
- Is there awareness of the constraints and tradeoffs that shaped current solutions?
- Does the critique of existing solutions show genuine understanding or naive dismissal?
- Is the founder's confidence based on insight or ignorance?

**Response Options:**
- Deep Understanding: Sophisticated grasp of why the landscape looks as it does; critique based on insight
- Good Understanding: Solid understanding of incumbent logic and constraints
- Basic Understanding: General awareness of current solutions without deep analysis
- Limited Understanding: Superficial view of existing solutions
- No Understanding: Naive dismissal of incumbents; critique based on ignorance

---

## Task 15: Holistic Innovation Assessment

**Purpose:** Provide an overall assessment of innovation quality that synthesizes all dimensions.

**Description:** This task considers all aspects together—conceptual novelty, technical and business model innovation, insight depth, cliché avoidance, and first-principles thinking—to determine whether the idea represents genuine innovation that could advance its field or define new categories.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Does the idea demonstrate genuine innovation across multiple dimensions?
- Is there a coherent vision that integrates novel elements into a compelling whole?
- Would this idea, if executed, represent a meaningful advance in how we solve problems or create value?
- Does the innovation feel authentic and earned rather than performed or superficial?

**Response Options:**
- Exceptional Innovation: Genuinely paradigm-shifting; could define new categories or fundamentally reshape an industry
- Strong Innovation: Meaningful innovation across multiple dimensions; could significantly advance its field
- Moderate Innovation: Solid innovation in at least one dimension; contributes something new to the landscape
- Limited Innovation: Some novel elements but largely within established patterns
- Minimal Innovation: Derivative concept with no meaningful innovation evident

---

## Scoring Notes

Each task produces a score that contributes to the overall innovation quality score. The function combines these individual assessments using weighted averaging or other aggregation methods to produce a final scalar score between 0 and 1.

The tasks are designed to be:
- **Independent**: Each evaluates a distinct dimension of innovation
- **Complementary**: Together they cover the full space of innovation quality
- **Robust**: Multiple perspectives prevent any single dimension from dominating inappropriately
- **Nuanced**: Response options capture meaningful gradations rather than binary judgments

The final score represents innovation quality, not likelihood of success. Highly innovative ideas may fail due to execution, timing, or market factors, while moderately innovative ideas with excellent execution may succeed. This function measures the quality of the innovative insight, which is one important but not sufficient factor in startup success.
